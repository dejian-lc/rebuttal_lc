# Venue mechanism adapter

## Runtime rule record

Record only task-relevant fields: venue/year/track or ARR cycle; stage; official source and retrieval date; effective date if stated; audience and visibility; reply count; size unit and counter; revision scope; new evidence; links/images/attachments; anonymity; chair/report channel; deadline and timezone. Unknown is a legitimate value.

Check the actual cycle announcement or submission interface for an exceptional change. Do not treat a general author guide as proof that no later override occurred. Do not infer unlimited exchanges from OpenReview, or prohibit all changes because another conference prohibited them. Distinguish numeric limits from advice and prohibitions from “may ignore” language.

If official sources conflict, record both statements and their locations. Prefer an explicit applicable update, but do not silently invent an update date. Continue unaffected drafting and seek clarification only when the disputed operation is necessary. Official notices supplied by the author are usable without forcing a redundant web search; verify live rules if relying on current policy knowledge.

## Verified examples of why the adapter matters

These are examples from official guide snapshots read on **2026-11-04**, not a complete current policy for a live submission. Check cycle-specific changes before using them.

### CVPR 2026

Source: https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines — “Rebuttal Policies.”

The guide specifies a one-page PDF in the official rebuttal template, anonymity, and exceptional confidential chair comments. Its new-evidence language discourages substantial requests and new contributions/results, particularly unsolicited ones; do not translate it into blanket permission for all requested experiments. The guide also contains general-link examples alongside stricter rebuttal-specific language; keep the response self-contained and check any proposed link against the rebuttal policy.

Consequence: prioritize a small number of decisive points, combine common concerns, and verify rendered page count. Do not assume an author discussion loop from the platform. A requested result still needs a relevance, scope, and policy check.

### ICML 2026

Source: https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorInstructions — “Reviewing and Author Response.”

This guide describes initial author responses visible to reviewers after the response period, followed by one additional round of communication. It does not permit a revised-paper upload during author feedback. Reviewers are not expected to follow external URLs. The retrieved section does not establish every numeric response limit.

Consequence: plan a self-contained initial response; reserve the available follow-up for residual questions. Draft proposed edits with accurate tense, not claims of uploading a revised paper. Check event-specific instructions for limits and allowed evidence.

### ICLR 2026

Source: https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuide — “Reviewing Process” and “FAQ During Discussion Stages.”

The general guide describes public discussion, manuscript revision with visible changes, per-comment limits without a stated comment-count cap, and reader-controlled comments. It says reviewers and chairs need not inspect every revision and can ignore substantial departures from the original paper. These general provisions do not establish whether a later special notice changed a particular stage.

Consequence: keep a versioned change summary, verify the active discussion permissions, and use brief substantive follow-ups. The general guide's flexibility does not justify extensive redesign or repeated nudges.

### ACL Rolling Review

Source: https://aclrollingreview.org/authors — “Author response,” “Review issue reporting,” and later meta-review FAQs.

The retrieved response section specifies one general response and one response per review, each capped at 5,000 characters; text only and no external links. It permits minor new experiments directly answering a reviewer, and excludes unsolicited new results or substantial new model work. Review problems are routed through a dedicated report stage. ARR reviews and meta-reviews inform a later commitment decision rather than themselves constituting conference acceptance.

**Unresolved internal inconsistency:** a later meta-review FAQ on the same snapshot still mentions a maximum of two author responses per thread, whereas the updated response section says one per review plus one general response. Use the stricter specific response section for a provisional plan and verify the active cycle notice/interface before any additional reply. Do not assert that the entire page is internally consistent.

The FAQ permits a polite request to reconsider evaluation while discouraging pressure; unchanged scores alone are not grounds for a complaint. Therefore “never mention evaluation” is not a universal venue prohibition. Prefer resolving the substance, and distinguish an allowed one-time request from repeated score lobbying.

## Journal and revision stages

When the task is a revise-and-resubmit response, use the editor's letter, deadline, revision requirements, and tracked-change conventions. Link replies to actual manuscript edits and identify any unfulfilled request with reasons. Do not impose a conference one-page economy on a journal point-by-point response. An acceptance-stage edit request is different from rebutting a review before a decision.
