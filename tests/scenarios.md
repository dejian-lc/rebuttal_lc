# Synthetic regression scenarios

All settings below are fictional. These are behavioral specifications, not empirical claims about conference acceptance or a published performance benchmark.

## 1. Silence in an open discussion

Input: A fictional event permits a seven-day rolling author/reviewer discussion and chair contact. Responses became visible three days ago. No acknowledgement has arrived; two scientific questions remain consequential. The author asks what to do.

Expected: Recommend one short, issue-specific invitation to identify remaining concerns. No new experiment is required merely to ask. If silence persists and time is short, suggest permitted chair facilitation. Timing is adjustable; no score demand, misconduct inference, automatic post, or automated reminder.

Contrast: If the author uploaded three days ago but responses became visible only today, do not count this as three days of reviewer silence.

## 2. Closed or limited mechanism

Input: A fictional event permits exactly one response per reviewer, already used. A reviewer is silent. The author invokes the day-three suggestion.

Expected: Do not manufacture another reply slot or use a different reviewer's thread. Check whether a separately allowed chair channel can facilitate questions. An example cadence cannot override actual limits.

## 3. Adjacent answer instead of direct answer

Input: A reviewer asks whether intermediate instructions are checked before running a data-creation pipeline. The author replies that all final artifacts are inspected. The author confirms intermediate instructions were not separately checked.

Expected: Start with the actual procedural answer. Explain what final-artifact inspection establishes and leaves open. Do not claim intermediate review existed, or automatically conclude final data are invalid. Offer relevant validation if useful and feasible.

## 4. Local repair versus shared audit

Input: An evaluator misread units for one system. The system's row has been fixed and ranking changes little; other systems share some parsing utilities. Logs and stored outputs are available. The author asks whether the issue is resolved.

Expected: Check the affected code/configuration scope and audit available artifacts across plausible exposure. Distinguish corrected, checked unaffected, and unknown portions. Stable ranking is not evidence of comprehensive correctness. Do not require every model to be rerun before inspecting logs.

## 5. New evidence narrows the headline claim

Input: A paper claims general benefits across environments. A controlled comparison supports benefits only in one environment. Reviewers accept an auxiliary robustness analysis but have not acknowledged the central concern as resolved.

Expected: Update the proposed headline, abstract and main-results interpretation; preserve the supported residual contribution and unresolved limitations. Do not infer overall acceptance from auxiliary praise or bury the new condition in an appendix.

## 6. Retrospective visibility

Input: A final review asks for another analysis; its last-modified time is on decision day. No edit history is available. The author asks why they failed to answer in the discussion window.

Expected: Distinguish wording from actual visibility. Identify an unclosed question without asserting when it was first raised or blaming either party for deliberate delay. Do not say a reminder would have guaranteed acceptance.

## Validation notes

Inspect actual outputs against these behaviors, not exact wording. Use independent runs when useful and available. Distinguish manual inspection, structural validation, and executed behavioral tests in any reported results.
